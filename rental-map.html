How ranges are set

Every area starts with a baseline range built from published averages (Zillow, Rentometer, RentHop, Zumper, RentCafe and Apartments.com), adjusted for the neighborhood. Published averages disagree a lot and lean toward large new buildings, so the baseline is only a starting point.

The real test is demand. We track live listings and divide the contacts each one gets by the days it has been listed:

  • More than 1.1 contacts a day means it is moving fast and the rent could go higher.
  • About 0.75 to 1.1 contacts a day means it is priced right.
  • Less than 0.75 contacts a day means it is sitting and is likely overpriced.

Once an area has listings in that priced-right window, their rents replace the baseline. Areas tuned this way have a green ring on the map. Listings under three days old aren't counted yet.

Small multifamily shows rent per unit in older 2–4 unit buildings (duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes). These units usually rent below a comparable apartment or house because they lack a private yard and have less space. Many share utility meters, so compare rents net of any utilities the owner pays. Track contacts per day for each vacant unit separately.

Condition, parking, laundry and utilities move rent inside any range. Treat these as the band where a typical, well-kept unit leases in a reasonable time.
